About

I'm a Life Sciences Engineering student at EPFL, somewhere between two paths I love — medicine and neuroscience — and slowly figuring out that the answer might just be both.

It started before EPFL. During a hematology internship at Semmelweis University Hospital in Budapest, I got my first real look at medicine from the inside, and left convinced that there was still so much to unlock — in how diseases are detected, and how the brain itself could be interfaced with technology. That conviction is what led me to life sciences engineering rather than directly to medical school: I wanted to understand both worlds deeply enough to help bridge them.

The question I keep coming back to is what actually shapes the way humans think, decide, and change. It has taken me from cognitive neuroscience research at EPFL's Laboratory of Cognitive Neuroscience (LNCO), to neurotech at Roceso Technologies in Singapore, and on to a medical-engineering exchange at KTH Stockholm and the Karolinska Institute — different contexts, same obsession: why the neural mechanisms driving human behaviour evolved the way they did, and how that knowledge can become tools in medicine and mental health.

The path I'm building leads toward neurosurgery and neurotechnology. I want to operate on the brain and build things that extend what surgery can do — not as competing ambitions, but as one coherent one. The kind of goal that takes twenty years and looks slightly unreasonable from the outside, which is usually a good sign.

Outside the lab, I'm quadrilingual (French, English, German, Hungarian), curious about most things, and especially the ones that don't have clean answers yet.

Research & Projects

Cognitive Neuroscience

Sense of Agency & Episodic Memory

Laidlaw research internship at EPFL's Laboratory of Cognitive Neuroscience. I built an online experimental application testing whether the sense of agency — the feeling of controlling one's actions — strengthens episodic memory, and added confidence ratings to the recognition protocol.

Read the research report →
Rehabilitation Robotics

Neurorehabilitation Robotics

R&D and clinical-operations internship at Roceso Technologies, Singapore — developing rehabilitation concepts for a hand-tracking device paired with a soft-robotic glove, grounded in agency–memory neuroscience, to help stroke patients recover.

Machine Learning

Computer Vision on Psychiatric Art

Led the deconfounding pipeline in a 48-hour clinical-AI pilot applying DINOv2 visual embeddings to 3,054 psychiatric patient artworks from Bern University Hospital — showing text inscription explains ~10× more visual variance than colour or figure content, and surfacing three expressive clusters mapped onto diagnoses.

Leadership

EPFL Sustainable Innovation Challenge

Challenge Team Leader for Europe's first student-led sustainability forum of its kind. Over 11 months I led the challenge design and the selection of teams and jurors, culminating in an international final at EPFL with finalist teams from Sweden, Poland, the Netherlands and Switzerland.

Neural Networks

Hopfield Networks & Learning Rules

An empirical study of Hopfield networks as associative memory — comparing Hebbian and Storkey learning rules, storage capacity and robustness to noise. Awarded top recognition in EPFL's BIO-210 programming competition (team of 3 among ~180 participants).

Software · C++

Population Simulation in C++

A dynamic ecosystem simulation in C++ with SFML: animals and plants in a 2D toroidal world with reproduction, predation, gestation and perception — built with polymorphism, inheritance and design patterns.
